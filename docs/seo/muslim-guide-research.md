## Halal / Muslim-friendly food (all self-declared — Mexico has no national halal certifier)
Playa del Carmen:
- Falafel Nassaya, Calle 6 Norte — states fully halal, no alcohol (Zabihah, reviews 2022 + Feb 2026) [1]
- Street Food Latino y Más, Av. Constituyentes 171 — separate halal menu on request, no alcohol (Zabihah, Mar 2026 review) [1]
- El Ranchito, 10 Av at Calle 22 — small halal lamb menu, serves alcohol [CONFLICT]
Cancún:
- Casa Persa / Moomba Lounge, Kukulcán km 9.5 — was listed fully halal; Zabihah says may have closed [CONFLICT]
- Habibi, Sahara Cocina Libanesa, 79th Element, Taste of India, Shawarma Express — HalalSpy only [1, weak]
- Resorts (Moon Palace, Hyatt Ziva halal meat on request; Margaritaville Island Reserve "halal meats") — HalalSpy only [1, weak]
Tulum: Tikka Curry & Grill — Google "temporarily closed", owner denied halal in 2022 review [CONFLICT]; Shams Arabic — location/halal unclear [CONFLICT]
Reliable fallback (repeated by sources): seafood and vegetarian; buffets risk pork/lard/alcohol cross-contamination.
Halal butcher/grocery: none found by any source.

## Population
- Mexico, INEGI 2020 census: 7,982 Muslims (2010: ~3,760) [2+]
- ~80% in Mexico City metro (Excélsior citing INEGI) [1]
- Quintana Roo: only an uncited 2010 figure (142) — do not publish
- Quintana Roo has Mexico's highest share of "no religion" (22.5%, 2020) — Mexico News Daily

## Practical
- All Mexican beaches are public by federal law (2020 reform, Ley General de Bienes Nacionales) [2+]; no swimwear rules found.
- Xcaret: no dress code; recommends long-sleeve rash guard; mineral sunscreen only [1]. No published burkini rule at Xcaret/Xel-Há/cenotes either way.
- Hacienda Real del Caribe (Playa, via HalalBooking): alcohol-free room, modest swimwear allowed at pool [1].
